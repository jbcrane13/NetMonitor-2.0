# NetMonitor 2.2.2 — release notes (draft; TestFlight candidate = iOS/widget build 20 / macOS 1795231846 with #435; builds 19 / 1795208316 added #433; builds 18 / 1795152348 are staged in App Store Connect, and 17 / 1794956703 were staged first and replaced)

It carries everything 2.2.1 staged (build 16 / macOS 1790565588, see `2.2.1.md`), the `release/2.2.2` branch (#337 review prompt, #334 macOS contrast pass), and the competitive-review epic #376. ## App Store "What's New" (user-facing)

> **Open (lead/Blake):** the lists below cover only changes since 2.2.1 build 16. Both App Store products are still live at 2.2.0; 2.2.1 was staged in App Store Connect but not submitted. If 2.2.1 never ships, add the highlights from `2.2.1.md`'s "What's New" to these lists before submitting.

### NetMonitor (iOS)
- A new app icon in the Liquid Glass style.
- Faster scans: NetMonitor stops waiting as soon as your network has answered, and finds Bonjour devices without opening a connection to each one.
- A device that has left the network now shows as Cached (dimmed in the device list and on the Network Map) instead of online, until it answers again.
- Device details show the exact model that Apple and other Bonjour devices announce, such as an Apple TV or HomePod model.
- Device details also show the maker that Bonjour devices announce, such as Apple or Sonos, along with the name and address from your latest scan.
- Smarter device alerts: a device that was only asleep no longer comes back as "new", and a device is reported missing only after it has been gone for several scans. The first scan of a network sets the baseline instead of reporting every device.
- New-device and target-down alerts are on from the first launch, as the settings always showed.
- New Health Checks card on the dashboard: router, DNS, internet, VPN, Wi-Fi signal, devices and link speed, each with a pass or warning and one plain sentence.
- DNS speed: Health Checks compares your current DNS server with Cloudflare, Google and Quad9 and shows the fastest.
- Scheduled speed tests (Settings › Speed Test, off by default) run every 1 to 24 hours while NetMonitor is open, and the Speed Test tool shows a 7-day trend of download, upload or ping.
- History retention now also covers timeline events, so old data is cleared as your retention setting says.
- After you join another network, such as at a hotel or a friend's house, the device list clears and NetMonitor scans the new network on its own. Going back home brings your home devices back, and Scan always scans the network you are on.
- The Internet Latency card shows each server's own ping time; before, Google, Cloudflare and Apple could all show the same number.

### NetMonitor Pro (macOS)
- A new app icon in the Liquid Glass style.
- Easier to read in light and dark mode: secondary text, chart labels, dividers and glass cards have stronger contrast across the dashboard, device views and tools.
- Faster scans: NetMonitor Pro stops waiting as soon as your network has answered, and finds Bonjour devices without opening a connection to each one.
- Routers, TVs, speakers and hubs that support UPnP now show their own name, maker and model.
- Device details show the exact model that Macs, Apple TVs, HomePods and other Bonjour devices announce, and that model helps NetMonitor Pro tell laptops, desktops and phones apart.
- Device lists show maker and model together, such as "Apple · Mac15,12", including the maker that Bonjour devices announce.
- The Network Health card shows your public IPv4 address and your router's address.
- The Devices toolbar fits its controls, so the network picker and view switcher show their labels.
- On macOS 15 through 26: a device that has left the network shows as Cached until it answers again; maker names come from a built-in list first, with an online lookup only when it doesn't know the maker; devices using a private Wi-Fi address are labelled "Private address", and keep their row when that address changes.
- Alerts: NetMonitor Pro now notifies you when a new device joins and when a monitored target goes down. A device that was only asleep no longer counts as new.
- Alert rules (Settings › Rules): "tell me when the printer goes offline". Pick an event (going offline, coming online, first seen, or latency above a limit) and, optionally, a device, device type or maker.
- Scan frequency, including Off, and "Retry failed checks" now take effect: NetMonitor Pro rescans on your schedule while open, and marks a target down only after the retries you set.
- History retention now also covers monitoring sessions and timeline events, and runs at launch.
- After your Mac joins another network, its device list shows that network's devices instead of the ones from the last network.
- New Health Checks card on the dashboard, with a DNS speed comparison against Cloudflare, Google and Quad9.
- Scheduled speed tests (Settings › Monitoring, off by default) every 1 to 24 hours, with a 7-day trend in the Speed Test tool.
- New Port Forwards tool (Tools › Discovery): a read-only list of the ports your router has opened through UPnP, with the device each one points to.

## Platform limits behind the App Store copy
- **macOS 27:** the sandboxed app reads no IPv4 ARP entries (#401), so scans get no MAC addresses and no ARP-only devices. Cached status (set only for ARP-only devices), maker names from the MAC, the "Private address" label and private-MAC row reuse don't appear there; discovery relies on TCP, Bonjour and SSDP. The Mac copy groups these under "On macOS 15 through 26".
- **Device change detection** keys by MAC, or by IP when there is none, so without MACs (iOS, macOS 27) an address change reads as a new device, not a change.
- **iOS alerts** from scheduled scans are new-device and missing-device only (`BackgroundTaskService`); there are no changed or came-back alerts and no rules on iOS.
- **Mac alerts:** the built-in new-device alert fires the first time a device answers; went-offline and came-back events reach the user only through Rules (#394), whose events are goes offline, comes online, first seen and latency above; changed events (#393) don't reach the user.
- **iOS:** the app sees the `02:00:00:00:00:00` placeholder instead of real MACs, so the offline maker list and "Private address" label are left out of the iOS copy. SSDP, UPnP names and Port Forwards need the multicast entitlement (#381) and are left out of the iOS copy.
- iOS has no model→type inference; it only shows the model.
